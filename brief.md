# Brief

## Question

If exceptional cultural achievements (Michelangelo, Bach, Shakespeare, Euler) enlarge what everyone afterward can perceive and produce, what exactly does education do with that inheritance, and does generative AI accelerate that work or counterfeit it?

## Claim

Genius is largely distributed across cultural infrastructure, the exemplars, tools, and possibility-spaces a learner inherits, and education is the process that converts that distributed genius into individual capability. The conversion, not the preservation, is the unsolved pedagogical problem: the cumulative-culture "ratchet" explains how gains are kept across generations but not how a collectively held achievement becomes an individual's usable, extendable capability. The paper's operational core is a distinction among three frontiers: the production frontier (what a learner can output with available tools), the competence frontier (what they can independently generate and revise), and the judgment frontier (what they can reliably evaluate). Generative AI expands the production frontier almost immediately while leaving competence and judgment comparatively unchanged; that gap is "false ratcheting," and it is revealed the moment the tool is removed. The distinction is measurable (test with vs without the tool), which is what makes the framework more than a metaphor. The paper states its predictions as falsifiable claims and ships an illustrative model that instantiates the definitions.

## Cornerstone literature

Must engage, and distinguish from, honestly:
- Cumulative culture / the ratchet: Tomasello, Kruger & Ratner (1993); Tennie, Call & Tomasello (2009); Boyd & Richerson (1985); Henrich (2016).
- Distributed / extended cognition and the systems view of creativity: Hutchins (1995); Clark & Chalmers (1998); Csikszentmihalyi (1999).
- Individual-effort accounts of expertise and their limits: Ericsson, Krampe & Tesch-Römer (1993); Macnamara, Hambrick & Oswald (2014).
- The design-condition literature: Collins, Brown & Newman (1989); Wood, Bruner & Ross (1976); Vygotsky (1978); Sweller (1988); Kapur (2008); Bjork & Bjork (2011); Kauffman (2000) for the adjacent possible.
- The AI-empirical anchors: Kestin et al. (2025) [the tutor RCT, cite carefully, do not overclaim]; Bastani et al. (2025); Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond (2025); Doshi & Hauser (2024); Shumailov et al. (2024).

What is genuinely new versus a renaming: the three-frontier decomposition and its operational tool-removal test; "generative regeneration" as the interactive transformation of a fixed exemplar; and "false ratcheting" as a measurable failure mode of AI-mediated learning. The neighbours (cognitive apprenticeship, desirable difficulties, productive failure, extended cognition) supply the design conditions and the distributed-cognition ontology; the contribution is the measurement that decides whether a tool ratchets genuinely.
