# Sources

## Bibliography

Bastani, H., Bastani, O., Sungu, A., Ge, H., Kabakcı, Ö., & Mariman, R. (2025). Generative AI without guardrails can harm learning: Evidence from high school mathematics. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences*, 122(26), Article e2422633122.
— Verified against PNAS (doi:10.1073/pnas.2422633122); peer-reviewed successor to the 2024 SSRN working paper "Generative AI Can Harm Learning." Supports false ratcheting: ~1,000 Turkish high-schoolers, +48% during unrestricted-GPT practice, −17% vs controls on the unassisted exam, harm removed by a guardrailed tutor.

Bjork, E. L., & Bjork, R. A. (2011). Making things hard on yourself, but in a good way: Creating desirable difficulties to enhance learning. In M. A. Gernsbacher, R. W. Pew, L. M. Hough, & J. R. Pomerantz (Eds.), *Psychology and the real world: Essays illustrating fundamental contributions to society* (pp. 56–64). Worth Publishers.
— Verified against the UCLA Bjork Lab publication list. Supports the design condition "preserve productive difficulty"; the desirable-difficulties construct the kernel encodes.

Boyd, R., & Richerson, P. J. (1985). *Culture and the evolutionary process*. University of Chicago Press.
— Verified (Univ. Chicago Press, ISBN 9780226069333). Supports the population-dynamic mechanism of cumulative cultural adaptation underlying the ratchet.

Brynjolfsson, E., Li, D., & Raymond, L. R. (2025). Generative AI at work. *The Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 140(2), 889–942.
— Verified against QJE (doi:10.1093/qje/qjae044); version of record of NBER WP 31161. Supports "the production frontier lifts most for the least skilled": ~15% average productivity gain, concentrated among less-experienced agents. (The 34%-for-novices figure is specific to the 2023 working paper; the published paper is cited and the novice effect described qualitatively.)

Clark, A., & Chalmers, D. (1998). The extended mind. *Analysis*, 58(1), 7–19.
— Verified against Oxford Academic (*Analysis*). Supports distributed genius: coupled external resources can be constitutive of cognition (active externalism, parity).

Collins, A., Brown, J. S., & Newman, S. E. (1989). Cognitive apprenticeship: Teaching the crafts of reading, writing, and mathematics. In L. B. Resnick (Ed.), *Knowing, learning, and instruction: Essays in honor of Robert Glaser* (pp. 453–494). Lawrence Erlbaum Associates.
— Verified (pp. 453–494). Supports decompression / making the expert process visible: modelling, coaching, scaffolding, articulation, reflection, exploration.

Csikszentmihalyi, M. (1999). Implications of a systems perspective for the study of creativity. In R. J. Sternberg (Ed.), *Handbook of creativity* (pp. 313–335). Cambridge University Press.
— Verified against Cambridge (pages conventionally 313–335). Supports "genius as environment": creativity is a property of a system of individual, domain, and field, not a lone trait.

Doshi, A. R., & Hauser, O. P. (2024). Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel content. *Science Advances*, 10(28), Article eadn5290.
— Verified (doi:10.1126/sciadv.adn5290). Supports the diversity paradox: AI story ideas raise individual creativity (most for less-creative writers) while making outputs more similar to one another.

Ericsson, K. A., Krampe, R. T., & Tesch-Römer, C. (1993). The role of deliberate practice in the acquisition of expert performance. *Psychological Review*, 100(3), 363–406.
— Verified (Vol. 100(3), 363–406). Supports the individual-effort account of expertise the paper qualifies (deliberate practice as effortful, feedback-rich training).

Henrich, J. (2016). *The secret of our success: How culture is driving human evolution, domesticating our species, and making us smarter*. Princeton University Press.
— Verified (Princeton UP, 2016). Supports the collective-brain / cumulative-culture thesis: human capability rests on accumulated transmitted know-how, not raw individual intelligence.

Hutchins, E. (1995). *Cognition in the wild*. MIT Press.
— Verified (MIT Press, 1995). Supports distributed cognition: a computational cognitive process carried across people, artifacts, and social organization.

Kapur, M. (2008). Productive failure. *Cognition and Instruction*, 26(3), 379–424.
— Verified (Vol. 26(3), 379–424). Supports the design condition "preserve productive difficulty": pre-instruction struggle yields deeper understanding and transfer.

Kauffman, S. A. (2000). *Investigations*. Oxford University Press.
— Verified (Oxford UP, 2000); canonical source for "the adjacent possible." Supports the possibility-space operator: innovation expands the set of states one step away from the current.

Kestin, G., Miller, K., Klales, A., Milbourne, T., & Ponti, G. (2025). AI tutoring outperforms in-class active learning: An RCT introducing a novel research-based design in an authentic educational setting. *Scientific Reports*, 15, Article 17458.
— Verified against *Scientific Reports* (doi:10.1038/s41598-025-97652-6). THE anchor RCT: N=194, effect 0.73–1.3 SD vs active learning, in less time. Cited as a single suggestive result; the paper carries the authors' OWN cautions (immediate/short-term post-lesson, heavily prompt-engineered scaffolded tutor, two physics topics, one course). Not presented as validating the framework.

Macnamara, B. N., Hambrick, D. Z., & Oswald, F. L. (2014). Deliberate practice and performance in music, games, sports, education, and professions: A meta-analysis. *Psychological Science*, 25(8), 1608–1618.
— Verified (Vol. 25(8), 1608–1618). Supports "capability sits in the environment, not only in practice hours": deliberate practice explains a modest, domain-dependent share of variance (26% games, 21% music, 18% sports, 4% education, <1% professions).

Shumailov, I., Shumaylov, Z., Zhao, Y., Papernot, N., Anderson, R., & Gal, Y. (2024). AI models collapse when trained on recursively generated data. *Nature*, 631, 755–759.
— Verified (Nature, 631, 755–759; a later author correction does not change the finding). Supports the archive-anchor warning as a precise analogy: recursive training on generated output loses distribution tails and variance.

Sweller, J. (1988). Cognitive load during problem solving: Effects on learning. *Cognitive Science*, 12(2), 257–285.
— Verified (Vol. 12(2), 257–285). Supports the overload arm of the kernel and worked-example logic: unguided problem solving can consume working memory without building schemas.

Tennie, C., Call, J., & Tomasello, M. (2009). Ratcheting up the ratchet: On the evolution of cumulative culture. *Philosophical Transactions of the Royal Society B*, 364(1528), 2405–2415.
— Verified (Vol. 364(1528), 2405–2415). The ratchet-effect statement: human culture uniquely accumulates modifications across generations.

Tomasello, M., Kruger, A. C., & Ratner, H. H. (1993). Cultural learning. *Behavioral and Brain Sciences*, 16(3), 495–511.
— Verified against Cambridge Core (Vol. 16(3); target article 495–511). Supports high-fidelity cultural transmission via imitative, instructed, and collaborative learning.

Vygotsky, L. S. (1978). *Mind in society: The development of higher psychological processes* (M. Cole, V. John-Steiner, S. Scribner, & E. Souberman, Eds.). Harvard University Press. (Original works written 1930–1934.)
— Verified editors and publisher; a compilation of essays written in the early 1930s (no single original year). Supports internalization and the zone of proximal development.

Wood, D., Bruner, J. S., & Ross, G. (1976). The role of tutoring in problem solving. *Journal of Child Psychology and Psychiatry*, 17(2), 89–100.
— Verified (Vol. 17(2), 89–100; doi:10.1111/j.1469-7610.1976.tb00381.x). Origin of "scaffolding": a tutor manages what the learner cannot yet, then withdraws support. Supports the fading condition.
